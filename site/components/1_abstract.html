Abstract

This paper tests the Temporal Equivalence Principle (TEP) through strong-lensing time-delay residuals, lensing–dynamical mass inference, and action-level photon-path refraction. SN Refsdal offers an unusually high-leverage test case: seven pre-reappearance lens-model variants spanning five modelling families published predictions for the long-baseline SX reappearance delay before the image was observed, and Kelly et al. (2023) later measured that delay independently from SN light-curve fitting. Six of seven strictly blind original predictions and all seven delay-blind revised predictions yield positive residuals (observed delay longer than predicted; correlation-aware family-sign-flip $p = 0.031$, strictly blind floor $p = 0.094$). Under a uniform time-delay rescaling, the $+30.1 \pm 8.9$ d residual corresponds to a target scale shift of $8.7\%$: stellar kinematics break the lens-model mass-sheet degeneracy, making the dynamical–lensing mass difference (phantom mass) a sign-testable consequence of the coupled reconstruction. For the multiply-imaged SLSN-I SN 2025wny ($z_s = 2.015$, five images A–E resolved by HST/JWST across a two-galaxy lens), whose delays were measured in August 2026, the predicted residual ($+2.3$ to $+7.1$ d on the measured $65.7\pm3.5$ d A–D baseline; Step 36b) is comparable to current lens-model precision ($\approx 6.8$ d), keeping the out-of-sample proxy test live.

At the action level (Step 64), the optical mechanism is resolved by comparing the two metric realizations on the selected two-branch kinetic sector $P_{,X}=k\sqrt\xi+2\xi$ ($k=16.03$ fixed by $a_0$, zero per-system free parameters). Under the historical isotropic-conformal benchmark $\tilde g_{\mu\nu}=A^2(\phi)g_{\mu\nu}$, spatial rulers stretch by $A^2(\phi)$ alongside clocks so $A^2(\phi(x))$ cancels out of $d\tilde s^2=0$, leaving only the Planck-suppressed scalar stress tensor ($\kappa_\phi\simeq6.5\times10^{-6}$ at 1 Mpc depth; $+0.246$ d on the Step 61 unit-term control), while GW170817-bounded disformal cone tilts ($B_0\lesssim78$) fall at least a factor $\simeq5$ short on the deprojected GLAFIC halo profile ($B_0^{\rm req}\simeq420$; Steps 62–63). Under the canonical unstretched-space temporal metric $\tilde g_{\mu\nu}=-A_{\rm loc}^2(u_{\rm grav}(\mathbf x))U_\mu U_\nu+A_{\rm sp}^2(u_{\rm drift}(t,\mathbf x))\Pi_{\mu\nu}$ ($U_\mu\equiv-\nabla_\mu\bar\phi/\sqrt{2\bar X}$) — where the $(3+1)$ ADM spacetime lapse rescales as $N_{\rm TEP}(x)=A(\phi(x))N_{\rm GR}(x)$ along the cosmic temporal flow while the spatial 3-metric $\gamma_{ij}$ remains unstretched — intermediate clock rates along the photon path do not cancel: every point $x$ contributes its local temporal depth $-\beta_A u_{\rm grav}(x)$ directly to the optical refractive index $n(x)\simeq1-2\Phi_N(x)/c^2-\beta_A u_{\rm grav}(x)$. Sourced by the cosmic baryon fraction alone ($f_b=0.16$, zero CDM), this unstretched-space temporal channel yields a 2-D sheet Fermat delay $\Delta t(\text{SX}-\text{S1})=+330.9$ d and a 3-D deprojected NFW ray delay of $+221.6$ d ($1\times$) to $+443.2$ d ($2\times$) in the exact observed arrival order $\text{SX}>\text{S1}>\text{S2}>\text{S3}>\text{S4}$, while preserving $c_{\rm GW}=c_\gamma$ identically on the shared temporal-lapse cone ($\Delta t_{\gamma-\rm GW}=0$) and satisfying Solar-System Shapiro bounds via quadratic-branch screening.

The probative signal is structurally concentrated in the S4–SX contrast: the inner Einstein cross provides negligible probative leverage under the adopted proxy. A transport audit establishes that the archived $\psi$ map measures the ordinary Fermat/Shapiro potential already included in the GR lens predictions; because isotropic $\tilde g_{\mu\nu}=A^2g_{\mu\nu}$ preserves null curves at fixed $g_{\mu\nu}$, it cannot be counted again as a static conformal residual. A separate hold-out test, calibrated only on SN Encore and the primary SN H0pe contrast with the common observed-delay covariance retained, predicts $+116.8\pm74.5$ d for Refsdal, statistically consistent with the covariance-corrected $+29.6\pm10.2$ d at $1.16\sigma$. Together with the unstretched-space temporal refraction solve (Step 64), the blind sign result and baryon-sourced delay scale provide a self-consistent optical and timing framework testable on the next long-baseline multiply-imaged supernova.
