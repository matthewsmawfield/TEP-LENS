5. Conclusion

A geometric blind-prediction residual test for the Temporal Equivalence Principle (TEP) has been applied to SN Refsdal (MACS J1149.6+2223), currently unique among cluster-lensed supernovae in combining five resolved images, a 376-day long-baseline delay, and an ensemble of pre-reappearance blind lens-model predictions. The key results are:

  1. The algebraic loop sum is identically zero under any theory with globally assignable arrival times per image, including the TEP ansatz. The probative observable is the blind-prediction residual (§1.1).
  2. The inner Einstein cross is a non-probative region under the adopted proxy (shear degeneracy collapses the magnification–convergence rank order; §4.1.1). All probative content resides in the S4–SX contrast.
  3. The two loops incorporating image SX—which arrives 376 days after S1—yield post-hoc proxy sensitivities of $-8.5$ days (S1–S2–SX) and $-14.5$ days (S1–S4–SX closure; $+14.5$ d obs−model) at nominal $\kappa_{\rm lens} = -0.055$. These define the nominal S4–SX response normalization used to compare sign and scale across the delay-blind ensemble. The 376-day baseline is the high-leverage contrast for the sign test.
  4. The algebraic loop sum is insensitive to the Mass Sheet Degeneracy: a uniform convergence sheet rescales all delays by the same factor, leaving the loop sum unchanged at zero under both GR and TEP. The blind-prediction residual is MSD-insensitive in the algebraic-loop sense, but remains lens-model-limited in practice because it compares observed delays to GR predictions that themselves carry mass-sheet and model-calibration uncertainties.
  5. The correlation-aware headline is the exact family-sign-flip test on method-family signs ($p=0.031$, $z \approx 1.86\sigma$), supported by independence-assuming Wilcoxon benchmarks ($p=0.0078$ delay-blind, $p=0.0039$ all eight). The strictly blind floor — original pre-reappearance predictions only, excluding the five post-reappearance revisions — gives $p = 0.094$ (6/7 positive, 4/5 families); the headline significance therefore rests on the delay-blind provenance of the revised values. A complementary directional-odds analysis gives one-sided Bayes factors ($\mathrm{BF}_{10}=56.8$ all non-zero; 31.9 delay-blind; 10.5 method-family-collapsed). Robustness checks preserve the directional pattern under model-dependence stress tests and microlensing-style flux perturbations, while hierarchical Bayesian comparison remains inconclusive (BF $\sim 1$), indicating that present lens-model uncertainties dominate formal model-selection metrics.
  6. Cross-system directional consistency. Blind-prediction residual tests for SN Encore and SN H0pe are directionally consistent with the proxy model: all three systems show the primary expected sign (3/3, exact binomial $p=0.125$). A normalization bug in the original four-contrast comparison has been corrected: each response is now divided by the coefficient actually used upstream. The uniform fractional model remains modestly preferred over the proxy ($\Delta$AIC $=1.12$). In the stricter one-contrast-per-system hold-out test with shared observational covariance, the corresponding difference is $\Delta$AIC $=1.23$.
  7. Internal consistency check — $H_0$ (not independent evidence). This is a calibrated response-scale estimate rather than an independent blind prediction: because $\kappa_{\rm lens}$ was calibrated from the same SN Refsdal SX delay data, applying it back to that same system cannot constitute an independent confirmation. Under the proxy model, the inferred $H_0$ shifts from $66.6$ to $69.3$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$ — a calibrated consequence of the empirical coupling, not corroborating evidence. SN Encore and SN H0pe receive only $\sim 0.1$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$ corrections (kappa calibrated on Refsdal and applied without refitting), too small to be probative. This $H_0$ shift is therefore reported only as an internal consistency consequence of the calibrated response scale, not as independent cosmological evidence for TEP.
  8. Across kinematics-calibrated (mass-sheet-broken) long-baseline multiply-imaged supernovae with comparable magnification contrast, microlensing control, and blind GR model uncertainty $\sigma_{\rm model} \lesssim 3.8$ d, an observed-minus-GR residual systematically consistent with zero at the $\lesssim 1$ d level would falsify the operational linear log-magnification proxy in the strong-lensing domain. Conversely, a residual matching the proxy-model prediction would constitute direct geometric evidence for potential-dependent temporal propagation once lens-model uncertainties are reduced sufficiently for high significance.
  9. Scalar-backreaction calculation and unstretched-space temporal refraction. Step 56 proves that the static isotropic-conformal $\psi$ integral is the GR Fermat/Shapiro term and cannot be added again as a TEP residual ($\Delta t^{\rm residual}_{\rm static,conf}=0$ under $\tilde g_{\mu\nu}=A^2g_{\mu\nu}$). Step 62 corrects the disformal bound transfer ($B_0\lesssim78$), and Step 63 performs the NFW+galaxy deprojection ($B_0^{\rm req}\simeq420$ at $r_s\simeq0.74$ Mpc). Step 64 then evaluates the selected two-branch kinetic sector $P_{,X}=k\sqrt\xi+2\xi$ ($k=16.03$ fixed by $a_0$, zero per-system free parameters): while isotropic conformal rescaling leaves only the Planck-suppressed scalar stress tensor ($\kappa_\phi\simeq6.5\times10^{-6}$), the unstretched-space temporal metric $\tilde g_{\mu\nu}=-A_{\rm loc}^2(u_{\rm grav}(\mathbf x))U_\mu U_\nu+A_{\rm sp}^2(u_{\rm drift}(t,\mathbf x))\Pi_{\mu\nu}$ ($U_\mu\equiv-\nabla_\mu\bar\phi/\sqrt{2\bar X}$) retains intermediate clock rates $A(\phi(x))$ along the ray, producing a $+330.9$ d 2-D sheet Fermat delay and $+221.6$ to $+443.2$ d 3-D deprojected NFW ray delay from $f_b=0.16$ baryons alone in the exact arrival order $\text{SX}>\text{S1}>\text{S2}>\text{S3}>\text{S4}$. These Step-64 values are absolute TEP long-baseline delays in the baryon-only reconstruction (demonstrating that the baryon-sourced temporal channel reaches the observed $\sim 376$ d SX–S1 delay scale and $\kappa(\text{SX})\simeq 0.20$ convergence with the correct arrival ordering), not additive corrections to the published GR delays; the $+30.1$ d ($8.7\%$) quantity is a separate residual diagnostic between observations and the published GR forecasts.
  10. The multiply-imaged SLSN-I SN 2025wny ($z_{\rm SN} = 2.0151$, five images A–E resolved by HST/JWST across two lens galaxies at $z = 0.3755$ and $0.3766$; Johansson et al. 2025; Goobar et al. 2026, arXiv:2608.28413) has measured delays published in August 2026 ($\Delta t_{AD}=-65.7\pm3.5$ d; arXiv:2608.28427, 2608.28416) and a space-data lens model ($H_0=66.7^{+7.6}_{-6.3}$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$ isothermal, $70.8^{+8.2}_{-6.1}$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$ free-slope; arXiv:2608.28430, with the isothermal value sitting within $0.21\sigma$ of TEP's zero-parameter cosmic root $H_0=68.17$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$ and shifting to $71.5$ km s$^{-1}$ Mpc$^{-1}$ under the median $+4.4$ d proxy delay correction, while the zero-parameter two-branch Gauss law on the $R_E=4.50$ kpc ring benchmark predicts $M_{\rm tot}(<R_E)/M_b(<R_E)=1.59$–$1.70$, i.e. a $37\%$–$41\%$ apparent dark-matter fraction inside $R_E$). On the measured $65.7\pm3.5$ d A–D baseline the proxy model predicts $+2.3$ to $+7.1$ d (Step 36b), comparable to the current $\approx6.8$-day lens-model precision: an external post-hoc test that sharper kinematics-calibrated lens models of this system can decide.

SN Refsdal provides the strongest current directional evidence for lensing-sector temporal-shear phenomenology: a pre-observation GR prediction ensemble, a later independent SX delay measurement, and a coherent positive residual sign pattern. The headline remains sign coherence ($p=0.031$). The non-circular hold-out amplitude test is statistically compatible but presently underpowered, while the static isotropic-conformal path-integral shortcut is excluded by exact null-curve invariance under $\tilde g_{\mu\nu}=A^2 g_{\mu\nu}$ and replaced by the unstretched-space temporal refraction closure of Step 64. The key theoretical task is to lock the coupled unstretched-space and lens-dynamics transfer function for prospective blind testing.

Quantitative analysis confirms this is a uniquely high-leverage single-system test: the probative content is concentrated in the S4–SX temporal lever arm (§4.1.1), with an effective dimensionality $D_{\rm eff} \approx 2.0$. The correlation-aware headline gives $p=0.031$ ($z \approx 1.86\sigma$), supported by independence-assuming Wilcoxon benchmarks; the strictly blind floor gives $p = 0.094$. All seven delay-blind-comparison residuals match the predicted sign, while the strict original-blind layer remains positive at six of seven. If the central Refsdal residual remains near the corrected-compilation value, reducing independent lens-model scatter below $\sigma_{\rm model} < 5$ d would move the amplitude test into a conclusive regime. Two additional systems (SN Encore, SN H0pe) show directional consistency with the proxy model. Statistical power scales with both model precision and time-delay baseline; refined kinematics-calibrated models of the five-image SN 2025wny system and additional long-baseline multiply-imaged supernovae with high magnification contrast will extend the test to high significance.

Together with the zero-per-system-parameter unstretched-space temporal refraction solve (item 9 above; §2.3, Step 64), which demonstrates that the baryon-sourced temporal channel reaches the observed long-baseline Fermat delay scale and convergence of SN Refsdal with the exact image arrival order while preserving $c_{\rm GW}=c_\gamma$ identically, these results establish a self-consistent optical and timing framework ready for prospective blind testing on the next long-baseline multiply-imaged supernova.
