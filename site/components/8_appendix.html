Appendix A: Theoretical Framework for Lensing Closures

A.1 Temporal Shear and the Lensing Potential

The Temporal Equivalence Principle (TEP) postulates that the rate of proper time flow for a photon traversing a gravitational potential $\Phi$ is scaled relative to the cosmological background rate by a factor $\Gamma_t$:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:8_appendix_01} \Gamma_t(\Phi) = 1 + \kappa_{\rm lens} \frac{|\Phi|}{c^2} \end{equation}

where $\kappa_{\rm lens}$ is the lensing-sector effective coupling. The proxy-model coefficient is empirically $\kappa_{\rm lens} \approx -0.055$; under the corrected Kelly+2023 Supplementary Table S4 compilation, the SN Refsdal data infer a lensing-sector coupling $\kappa_{\rm inferred} = -0.114 \pm 0.034$, which exceeds the proxy value by $1.75\sigma$. In the strong lensing regime, the relevant potential is the projected gravitational potential $\psi(\boldsymbol{\theta})$ integrated along the line of sight. For singular isothermal sphere (SIS) or NFW profiles typical of cluster lenses, the projected potential depth scales logarithmically with the surface mass density $\Sigma$, which is directly related to the magnification $\mu$.

The endpoint expression above is a clock-ratio diagnostic, not by itself an additional arrival-time observable. In the strictly static conformal sector, $\tilde g_{\mu\nu}=A^2(\phi)g_{\mu\nu}$, null propagation obeys the exact identity

\begin{equation} \label{eq:8_appendix_01b} \tilde g_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu=A^2(\phi)g_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu=0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad g_{\mu\nu}k^\mu k^\nu=0\,. \end{equation}

Consequently, conformal rescaling preserves the unparameterised null trajectories and their static coordinate travel times. The thin-lens relation $\int |\Phi|c^{-2}dl=(D_lD_s/2D_{ls})\psi(\boldsymbol{\theta}_i)$ is the ordinary potential part of the GR Fermat delay already contained in the lens predictions. Adding a fitted multiple of this term after subtracting those predictions double-counts the same potential. Step 56 therefore gives $\Delta t^{\rm residual}_{\rm static,conf}=0$ exactly; the archived $\psi$ ordering is retained only as a lens-environment diagnostic.

Any non-zero TEP lensing residual must therefore enter through a channel absent from this static conformal calculation: scalar backreaction on the solved lens geometry, explicit time dependence, or the disformal term $B(\phi)\nabla_\mu\phi\nabla_\nu\phi$. Step 61 evaluates the first channel from the action: identifying $\alpha(\phi)$ with the scalar self-energy lensing potential $\Psi_\phi$ on the GLAFIC mass map yields $\kappa_\phi \approx 4\times10^{-5}$ and a SX–S1 differential delay of $+0.25\,$d — correct sign, but $\sim 2\times10^3$ short of the phantom mass the observed residual requires. Direct scalar curvature of the photon path is thereby foreclosed as the residual's carrier; the admissible routes are the dynamical–lensing (phantom-mass) channel of §4.4, in which the scalar modifies the fitted model's mass inference, and the disformal transport term. For the latter, Step 62 corrects an earlier bound-transfer error: the GW170817 constraint bounds the coupling $B_0$ through the peak of the cone-split functional on the GW host's galactic profile, not the local tilt on the cluster path. Step 63 then performs the deprojection the channel's status depended on: the cluster-scale centroid is recovered from the smoothed $\kappa$ map (the unsmoothed $\kappa$-weighted centroid locks onto the host-galaxy spike; the earlier psi-map argmax sits at the map edge), a spherical NFW + constant background is fitted to the azimuthal profile (scatter $\simeq4\%$), the host galaxy is assigned its aperture mass excess — measured to be zero above the smooth halo, so the result is unchanged with or without a galaxy component — and the three-dimensional field $g_z(z)$ is integrated along each image's line of sight ($b_{\rm cl} = 44$ kpc for SX versus $68$–$81$ kpc for S1–S4). The model reproduces the tabulated image $\kappa$ values to $\sim5\%$ including the SX-deepest ordering. The required coupling is $B_0^{\rm req}\simeq420$ at the best-fit halo scale $r_s\simeq0.74$ Mpc — a factor $\simeq5$ above the GW170817 envelope $B_0\lesssim78$ — with the sign correct (SX delayed more than S1). Bound-crossing requires coherent line-of-sight extents $\gtrsim1.8$ Mpc ($B_0^{\rm req}\simeq70$ at $r_s=2$ Mpc), beyond standard cluster concentrations — and at such scales the azimuthal fit demands an unphysical negative background surface density ($b_{\rm g}<0$ for $r_s\gtrsim0.9$ Mpc), so even the nominal crossing is not attained within a physical deprojection. The channel is therefore bounded a factor $\sim5$ short under the measured halo deprojection rather than being a boundary coincidence. This $\times5$ figure is the weakest pairing of the requirement with any extant bound: under the uniform-$B_0$ reading the realized drift-transit lapse wall ($B_0\lesssim0.03$ at the absorber-epoch ambients, Paper 0 Step 52; Paper 29 Gate 10-c) tightens the shortfall to $\sim10^{4}$, and under the total-field convention — $B(u)$ evaluated at the cosmological ambient $u_{\rm amb}(z_l)=\ln(1+z_l)\simeq0.43$ — the enhancement of ${\rm shape}(u)$ on the lens path ($0.155$ versus $9\times10^{-9}$) is outrun by the same convention applied to the multimessenger path, where the drift-gradient term $(\dot u R_H)^2=1$ bounds $B_0\lesssim3\times10^{-11}$ against a requirement $B_0^{\rm req}\simeq2.3\times10^{-5}$. Every consistent convention forecloses the channel for a uniform coupling; the perturbation-sector pairing is retained as the conservative headline. One form remains open by construction, and it is the only form the corpus admits: the environment-modulated coupling $B_{\rm eff}=B(\phi)\,G(X_{\rm local})$ that the absorber-amplitude and ambient-lapse ledgers independently select (Paper 29 Gates 10-b/10-c; Paper 0 step 52). Evaluated under that form — the gate closed on the ambient and saturating above the DLA depth scale, $G\sim10^{-3}$ inside nonlinear wells — the same deprojected path integral returns $B_0^{\rm req}\simeq0.02$ (equivalently $B_0\,G_{\rm sat}\simeq2.3\times10^{-5}$), but the same gated convention applied to the step-50 GW170817 host profile gives $B_0^{\rm max}\simeq1.3\times10^{-4}$: the host's ambient-field shape enhancement outruns its partial gate closure, so the channel is bounded $\sim176\times$ short under the modulated coupling as well — rising to $\sim10^{3}$ at the activation depth $\lesssim10^{-7}$ the absorber phenomenology itself requires, and invariant under the saturation level $G_{\rm sat}$, which cancels between the requirement and the bound (Step 63, admissible-gated variant). The exclusion is therefore not specific to the uniform reading; it holds under every consistent convention evaluated to date. The following relation is accordingly tested only as a phenomenological strong-lensing ansatz, with its amplitude subjected to the external holdout test of Step 57.

\begin{equation} \label{eq:8_appendix_02} \Gamma_t(i) \approx 1 + \kappa_{\rm lens} \log_{10}\left(\frac{\mu_i}{\bar{\mu}}\right) \end{equation}

where $\mu_i$ is the absolute magnification of image $i$, and $\bar{\mu}$ is the mean magnification of the image system. This logarithmic scaling captures the essential feature that temporal shear is differential: it depends on the ratio of potential depths probed by different images. The test is a screen of this ansatz, not a measurement of the fundamental coupling.

A.2 Derivation of the Predicted GR Discrepancy

Let $t_i^{\rm GR}$ be the arrival time of image $i$ predicted by General Relativity and $t_0$ the unlensed arrival time. Define the GR absolute delay $u_i = t_i^{\rm GR} - t_0$ and the GR pairwise delay $\Delta t_{ij}^{\rm GR} = t_j^{\rm GR} - t_i^{\rm GR} = u_j - u_i$. Under TEP, the observed arrival time is scaled by the path-specific shear $\Gamma_i$:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:8_appendix_03} t_i^{\rm obs} = t_0 + \Gamma_i (t_i^{\rm GR} - t_0) = t_0 + \Gamma_i u_i \end{equation}

The observed pairwise delay between images $i$ and $j$ is therefore:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:8_appendix_04} \Delta t_{ij}^{\rm obs} = t_j^{\rm obs} - t_i^{\rm obs} = \Gamma_j u_j - \Gamma_i u_i \end{equation}

Because observed arrival times are physical clock readings, the sum of observed pairwise delays around any closed loop is identically zero:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:8_appendix_05} \Delta t_{ij}^{\rm obs} + \Delta t_{jk}^{\rm obs} + \Delta t_{ki}^{\rm obs} = (\Gamma_j u_j - \Gamma_i u_i) + (\Gamma_k u_k - \Gamma_j u_j) + (\Gamma_i u_i - \Gamma_k u_k) = 0 \end{equation}

The TEP signature is therefore not a failure of the observed delays to close, but a discrepancy between the observed delays and the delays predicted by GR lens models. The TEP predicted GR discrepancy is defined as the weighted sum of GR pairwise delays with coefficients $(\Gamma_m - 1)$:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:8_appendix_06} \mathcal{R}_{\rm TEP}(i,j,k) \equiv (\Gamma_i - 1)\,\Delta t_{ij}^{\rm GR} + (\Gamma_j - 1)\,\Delta t_{jk}^{\rm GR} + (\Gamma_k - 1)\,\Delta t_{ki}^{\rm GR} \end{equation}

Substituting $\Delta t_{ij}^{\rm GR} = u_j - u_i$ and expanding:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:8_appendix_07} \mathcal{R}_{\rm TEP} = (\Gamma_i - 1)(u_j - u_i) + (\Gamma_j - 1)(u_k - u_j) + (\Gamma_k - 1)(u_i - u_k) \end{equation}

Grouping terms by absolute delay:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:8_appendix_08} \mathcal{R}_{\rm TEP} = u_i(\Gamma_k - \Gamma_i) + u_j(\Gamma_i - \Gamma_j) + u_k(\Gamma_j - \Gamma_k) \end{equation}

This can also be written in the pairwise-delay form used in the main text by expanding $\Gamma = 1 + \delta\Gamma$ and using $\sum_{\rm loop}\Delta t^{\rm GR} = 0$:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:8_appendix_09} \mathcal{R}_{\rm TEP} = \sum_{\rm loop} (1 + \delta\Gamma)\,\Delta t^{\rm GR} = \sum \Delta t^{\rm GR} + \sum \delta\Gamma\,\Delta t^{\rm GR} = \sum_{\rm loop} (\Gamma_m - 1)\,\Delta t_{mn}^{\rm GR} \end{equation}

Explicitly for the triplet $(i, j, k)$:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:8_appendix_10} \mathcal{R}_{\rm TEP} = (\Gamma_i-1)\Delta t_{ij} + (\Gamma_j-1)\Delta t_{jk} + (\Gamma_k-1)\Delta t_{ki} \end{equation}

where $\Delta t_{ij} \equiv \Delta t_{ij}^{\rm GR}$. This equation demonstrates that the residual is generated purely by the differences in $\Gamma$ across the oriented image triplet. If the potential depth is constant ($\Gamma_i = \Gamma_j = \Gamma_k$), the residual vanishes. For the S1–S4–SX loop of SN Refsdal, where the other two delays are well-constrained by the Einstein-cross images, this weighted sum approximately equals the TEP-induced discrepancy in the SX–S1 delay. This is not an observed delay-closure violation. It is a model-space residual combination comparing GR-predicted delays to a TEP-scaled reference. Algebraic closure of pairwise delays remains exactly zero; the genuine TEP observable is the blind-prediction residual, not a synchronization holonomy.

A.4 Immunity to Mass Sheet Degeneracy

The Mass Sheet Degeneracy (MSD) corresponds to a transformation of the convergence $\kappa \to \lambda \kappa + (1-\lambda)$, which rescales time delays by a factor $\lambda$:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:8_appendix_11} \Delta t_{ij}' = \lambda \Delta t_{ij} \end{equation}

Substituting this into the predicted GR discrepancy definition:

\begin{equation} \label{eq:8_appendix_12} \mathcal{R}' = \Delta t_{ij}' + \Delta t_{jk}' + \Delta t_{ki}' = \lambda(\Delta t_{ij} + \Delta t_{jk} + \Delta t_{ki}) \end{equation}

Since $\Delta t_{ij} + \Delta t_{jk} + \Delta t_{ki} = 0$ in GR, then $\mathcal{R}' = \lambda \cdot 0 = 0$.

Thus, a uniform Mass Sheet Degeneracy cannot generate a non-zero algebraic loop sum by itself, because it rescales all delays coherently. This does not imply that the empirical blind-prediction residual is immune to all lens-model systematics. The narrower result is that the temporal-shear residual tested here is not algebraically equivalent to a trivial uniform mass-sheet rescaling.
